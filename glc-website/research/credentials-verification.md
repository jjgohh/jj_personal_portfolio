# Credentials verification — what may be published, and what may not yet

The brief is explicit: *"Do not assert a tender limit from memory — verify it, and if you can't verify it, leave it out rather than guess."* This file records, for every credential claim the site might make, the source and the verification status. **Nothing marked NOT VERIFIED may appear on a page.**

## Verification was only partly possible

Page fetching is blocked in this environment (see `research/reference-audit.md`). `cidb.gov.my` and `pdp.gov.my` could not be opened, so **no claim below is confirmed against the primary source with a verbatim quote.** Web search worked, so I can report what secondary sources say and how consistently they say it, but a search snippet is not CIDB's published source and the brief does not accept it as one.

Practical consequence: the Credentials page ships in Phase 2 stating only the things printed on GLC's own certificates, which are primary documents in the family's possession. The interpretive claims — what a G6 grade permits, what 3 stars signifies — wait until someone with web access confirms them. The page is designed so those sentences can be added later without a redesign.

Two notes on this tier:

- The brief lists the CIDB registration and the Sijil Perolehan Kerja Kerajaan as two separate credentials. The site must not imply that the MOF registration qualifies GLC to tender for government building works — that is what the CIDB SPKK does.

## Tier 3 — credentials GLC does not have (never imply)

Confirmed absent from the entire 65-page profile: any ISO certification (9001, 14001, 45001), any QLASSIC assessment, any SHASSIC assessment, any safety record or accident statistics, any award, any professional-body membership, any accreditation beyond CIDB and MOF. The brief notes that GT Max and TCS both advertise ISO certifications. GLC must not imitate, gesture at, or leave room to infer any of them. No "quality assured" or "safety first" copy blocks either — those read as ISO-adjacent claims and they are unbacked.

## Malaysian PDPA — the privacy notice requirement

Same limitation: `pdp.gov.my` is unreachable, so none of this is confirmed against the primary source.

What search establishes, consistently across several law-firm summaries: the **Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024** is being brought into force in phases, with the main provisions commencing **1 January 2025** and the data breach notification and data protection officer provisions commencing **1 June 2025**. The Personal Data Protection Department has issued three supporting guidelines: on data breach notification, on the appointment of a data protection officer, and Guidelines No. 03/2025 on cross-border personal data transfer. The amendments introduced a breach-notification duty to the Commissioner "as soon as practicable", notification to affected individuals where a breach is likely to cause significant harm, a right to data portability, and DPO appointment obligations.

Search did **not** establish two things the brief needs:

- Whether a bilingual notice (Bahasa Malaysia and English) is legally required. The original section 7 Notice and Choice Principle has generally been understood to require both languages, but I could not confirm the current wording. **Write the notice in both languages regardless** — it is right for this audience and it removes the question.
- Whether a small building contractor falls within a prescribed registration class of data user. **Ask a Malaysian lawyer or check with the PDP Department.** This is a compliance question with a penalty attached and it should not be settled from a search result.

What the Phase 2 privacy notice will cover, which is safe on any reading: what the enquiry form collects and why; that the WhatsApp link opens a conversation on WhatsApp under WhatsApp's own terms; who the data is shared with (the form provider as a processor, nobody else); how long enquiries are kept; how to ask for access, correction or deletion; and a named contact. Plus a decision on whether any analytics runs at all — the honest answer for this site is probably none, which removes a whole section of the notice.

## To close this file out

One person with ordinary web access, about 45 minutes:

1. Open CIDB's contractor-registration pages and CIMS. Confirm claims 1, 4, 6 and 7 with verbatim quotes and the page URL. Note the publication or revision date of whatever you quote.
2. Confirm whether the 3-star SCORE minimum is a live requirement, and from what date.
3. Ask the family for the PPK certificate and its expiry date.
4. Open `pdp.gov.my`, confirm the section 7 notice requirements and whether a contractor must register as a data user.
5. Move each confirmed claim into Tier 1 with its quote and URL, and delete it from Tier 2. Anything still unconfirmed stays off the site.
