# The logo, and the honest answer on trade marks

## 1. Read this part first

You asked me to make sure nobody else has used the logo, so you don't run into
copyright or trade mark problems. I need to separate those two things, because
I can give you a real answer on one and only a partial one on the other.

**Copyright in the artwork: low risk, and I can explain exactly why.** The mark
is three rectangles placed on a stated 64-unit grid, generated by
`tools/make_logo.py`, which you can read. Nothing was traced, sampled, adapted
or redrawn from any existing logo. The lettering is Archivo, released under the
SIL Open Font License, which expressly permits using the outlines in a logo. So
there is no third party whose drawing this could be said to copy, and the
construction file is your evidence of that if anyone ever asks.

**Trade marks: I have not cleared this, and I cannot.** Two reasons, both real:

1. **I could not reach any trade mark register from here.** I tried MyIPO's
   search system and this environment's network policy blocked it, the same way
   it blocked CIDB's website earlier in this project. So I have searched
   precisely nothing.
2. **Even with full internet access, a search by me would not be a clearance.**
   Trade mark clearance is a legal opinion about whether a mark is confusingly
   similar to earlier rights for related goods and services. It covers
   registered marks, pending applications, and unregistered marks with
   reputation, and it turns on judgement about similarity that a database
   lookup does not settle. That is a job for a registered trade mark agent.

So please do not treat this logo as cleared. Treat it as an original design that
is ready to be cleared. Section 5 tells you how, and it is neither slow nor
expensive.

## 2. The mark

A portal frame standing on the ground.

- The **navy column and beam** are the frame: the thing you build. It is the
  same shape as the steel portal frames in your own photographs of the Sungai
  Buloh and Bukit Raja warehouses.
- The **cyan bar** is the ground, and it deliberately runs wider than the frame
  on both sides. That is the "Land" in Global Land Consortium: the ground comes
  first and extends beyond what is built on it.
- Read as a whole it is also a building section, which is the drawing every one
  of your projects starts from.

It is not a globe. "Global" almost always becomes a globe, and a globe is the
single most crowded device in any trade mark register, so it is both a cliché
and the worst possible collision risk. It is not a house outline or a hard hat
either, for the same reason.

Colour comes from where it already came from on the website: the cyan is
sampled from your own company-profile cover, and the navy from the blue steel
cladding in your warehouse photographs.

**Construction, so it can be rebuilt exactly.** On a 64-unit grid:

| Element | x | y | width | height |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground bar (cyan `#00A0E0`) | 3 | 47 | 58 | 11 |
| Beam (navy `#14212F`) | 11 | 9 | 42 | 11 |
| Column (navy `#14212F`) | 11 | 9 | 11 | 38 |

Drawn extent x3–61, y9–58. One stroke weight of 11 units throughout. No curves,
no gradients, no effects, so it reproduces identically in vinyl cut, embroidery,
engraving, a rubber stamp and a one-colour newspaper advertisement.

## 3. Why the mark, and not "GLC"

I made a set of the initials as an alternative (`glc-wordmark-initials.svg`) so
you can see it, but I recommend against leading with it, for three reasons.

**In Malaysia, GLC already means something else.** GLC is the standard shorthand
for a *government-linked company* — Khazanah, PNB and Minister of Finance Inc
portfolio companies. Your third audience is government and GLC procurement
officers. A private Sdn Bhd contractor whose logo says GLC is going to read, to
exactly those readers, as claiming to be something it is not. That is a
positioning problem before it is ever a legal one.

**Three letters are weak to own.** Short letter marks are the most crowded part
of any register, and GLC in particular is used commercially elsewhere, including
as a Mercedes-Benz vehicle line. Different goods, so not necessarily a conflict,
but it means the initials alone will never be distinctive to you.

**The full name is the asset.** "Global Land Consortium Sdn Bhd" with a
registration number is what appears on your CIDB certificate, your MOF
certificate and every tender you submit. Procurement officers match documents.
Lead with the name, and let the initials be an informal nickname that never
carries the brand on its own.

## 5. Using it properly

**Clear space.** Keep space equal to the height of the cyan ground bar clear on
all four sides. Nothing sits inside that.

**Minimum size.** The horizontal lockup: 32 mm wide in print, 150 px on screen.
Below that the registration number stops being legible, so use the mark alone or
the badge.

**Backgrounds.** Navy or white for preference. On a photograph, use the reversed
version and only over a genuinely dark, quiet area — never over a busy site
photo where the frame disappears into scaffolding.

**Do not:** add a gradient, a drop shadow, an outline or a bevel; rotate it;
stretch it to fit a space; change the two colours; put the mark inside a circle;
re-set the name in a different typeface; or place the cyan bar on top of the
frame. The bar is the ground. It goes underneath.

## 6. What to do about the trade mark, in order

None of this needs a lawyer to begin with, and the first two steps are free.

1. **Search MyIPO yourself, first.** The Intellectual Property Corporation of
   Malaysia runs a public online search. Search the words *Global Land
   Consortium*, then *Global Land*, then the initials *GLC*, and filter to
   **Class 37**, which is the Nice classification class covering building
   construction, repair and installation services. That is the class you would
   file in. Ten minutes, and it will tell you immediately if there is something
   obvious.
2. **Search the SSM company register too.** A company name that is already
   registered is not a trade mark, but a similar builder's name in the Klang
   Valley is worth knowing about before you print signboards.
3. **Then engage a registered trade mark agent.** In Malaysia only a registered
   agent can file on your behalf if you are not filing yourself. Ask them for a
   clearance search and an opinion covering Class 37, and ask whether Class 42
   is worth adding. Get the fee in writing; for a single class this is a modest
   professional fee plus the official filing fee, not a large project.
4. **File the composite, not just the words.** Your agent will likely advise
   this anyway: apply for the logo together with "Global Land Consortium",
   because on their own the words *Global* and *Consortium* are weak and
   partly descriptive, and a simple geometric device on its own can attract a
   distinctiveness objection. The two together are much stronger than either
   alone.
5. **Do not apply for "GLC" alone.** See section 3.
6. **Keep using it while the application runs.** You can use the mark from day
   one. Registration takes months in any jurisdiction, and use builds rights of
   its own. Just do not put an ® next to it until it is actually registered —
   that is an offence. If you want to mark it before then, ™ is fine and needs
   no registration.

## 7. What I would still check before printing signboards

Two things I could not do from here, and neither is expensive:

- **A reverse image search on the mark**, to see whether a similar Γ-on-a-bar
  device is already in use by a construction company anywhere. Save
  `glc-icon-512.png` and drop it into Google Images or TinEye. Five minutes.
- **A look at the other Malaysian G6 and G7 contractors' logos**, which was also
  on the list I could not complete for the reference audit. If a competitor in
  the Klang Valley is using something close, you want to know before the
  hoarding is printed, not after.

If either turns up something similar, tell me and I will redraw it. The mark is
generated from nine numbers in one file, so a change is quick.
