A portal frame standing on the ground.

A logo for Global Land Consortium Sdn Bhd, built from three rectangles on a 64-unit grid. Original artwork, not yet cleared for trade mark — the honest position is at the bottom of this page.

What it is

The markNavy column and beam: the frame you build, the same shape as the steel portal frames in your Sungai Buloh and Bukit Raja photographs. Cyan bar: the ground, running wider than the frame on both sides. That overhang is the "Land" in the name — the ground comes first and extends past what is built on it.

ReversedFor navy and for photographs. Only over a genuinely dark, quiet area — never over a busy site photo where the frame disappears into the scaffolding.

It is deliberately not a globe. "Global" almost always becomes a globe, and a globe is the most crowded device in any trade mark register: a cliché and the worst collision risk at the same time. No house outline and no hard hat either, for the same reason.

Colour

Brand cyan#00A0E0 From your deck cover
Navy#14212F From your steel cladding
Deep cyan#0A6485 Links and buttons on light
Paper#EDEFF1Page ground

Two brand colours and nothing else. The bright cyan is illegible as text on a light ground, so it carries the navy bands and the deep cyan carries links and buttons. Every pair on the website was contrast-checked against WCAG AA rather than judged by eye.

Using it properly

Clear space. Keep space equal to the height of the cyan ground bar clear on all four sides. Nothing sits inside that.

Minimum size. The horizontal lockup: 32 mm wide in print, 150 px on screen. Below that the registration number stops being legible — use the mark alone or the badge instead.

Backgrounds. Navy or white for preference.

Do not

Add a gradient, a drop shadow, an outline or a bevel. Rotate it. Stretch it to fit a space. Change the two colours. Put the mark inside a circle. Re-set the name in a different typeface. Or place the cyan bar on top of the frame — the bar is the ground, it goes underneath.

Why not "GLC"

In Malaysia, GLC already means government-linked company. It is the standard shorthand for the Khazanah, PNB and Minister of Finance Inc portfolio companies. One of the three audiences for this website is government and GLC procurement officers. A private Sdn Bhd contractor whose logo says GLC will read, to exactly those readers, as claiming to be something it is not. That is a positioning problem before it is ever a legal one.

Three letters are weak to own. Short letter marks are the most crowded part of any register, and GLC is used commercially elsewhere, including as a Mercedes-Benz vehicle line. Different goods, so not necessarily a conflict — but it means the initials alone will never be distinctive to you.

The full name is the asset. "Global Land Consortium Sdn Bhd" with the registration number is what appears on your CIDB certificate, your MOF certificate and every tender you submit. Procurement officers match documents. Lead with the name; let the initials stay an informal nickname.

The honest answer on trade marks

This artwork is original. It is not cleared, and I could not clear it.

Copyright in the artwork: low risk. The mark is three rectangles on a stated grid, generated by a script you can read. Nothing was traced, sampled or adapted from any existing logo, and the lettering is Archivo under the SIL Open Font License, which permits exactly this. The construction file is your evidence if anyone asks.

Trade marks: I have searched nothing. I tried MyIPO's search system and this environment's network policy blocked it, the same way it blocked CIDB's website earlier in this project. And even with full access, a database lookup by me would not be a clearance: that is a legal opinion about confusing similarity with earlier rights, and it belongs to a registered trade mark agent.

  1. Search MyIPO yourself. Free, ten minutes. Search "Global Land Consortium", then "Global Land", then "GLC", filtered to Class 37 — building construction, repair and installation services. That is the class you would file in.
  2. Search the SSM company register too. Not a trade mark, but a similar builder's name in the Klang Valley is worth knowing before signboards are printed.
  3. Reverse image search the mark. Drop the 512px icon into Google Images or TinEye. Five minutes. Tell me if anything close comes back and I will redraw it — the mark is nine numbers in one file.
  4. Then engage a registered trade mark agent for a clearance search and an opinion on Class 37, and ask whether Class 42 is worth adding.
  5. File the logo together with the words, not either alone. On their own "Global" and "Consortium" are weak and partly descriptive, and a simple geometric device can attract a distinctiveness objection. Together they are much stronger.
  6. Use it now, but no ® until it is registered. Using the ® symbol before registration is an offence. ™ is fine and needs nothing.
